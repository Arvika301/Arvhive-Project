Conventional maps can record territory and rehabilitation plans can record affected populations, but neither alone preserves how displacement transforms place, memory and language. This archive connects multiple forms of evidence within one relational system.

RESEARCH QUESTION

How can a digital spatial-linguistic archive reveal forms of displacement that conventional maps and rehabilitation records fail to preserve?

The archive tests this question by placing spatial, institutional, visual, audiovisual and linguistic evidence in relation rather than treating any single source as complete.

04 / THROUGH TIME

A century beneath
the burning ground.

Jharia's present landscape is the result of overlapping histories of coal extraction, underground fire, land instability, state intervention and rehabilitation.

1890s
EXTRACTION / LAND

Expansion of private mining

Private mine ownership and expanding coal extraction shaped the early industrial landscape of the Jharia Coalfield.

1916
FIRE / ENVIRONMENT

Mine fire documented

BCCL records the outbreak of coal-mine fire in the Jharia Coalfield as early as 1916. Fire would become one of the defining conditions of the region's changing landscape.

1929
GEOLOGICAL MAPPING / ARCHIVE EVIDENCE

Mapping the coalfield

A Geological Survey of India map provides an early cartographic record of the Jharia Coalfield, allowing later fire and displacement evidence to be read against an earlier geological representation.

1971–73
NATIONALISATION / GOVERNANCE

A new institutional landscape

India's coal industry entered the nationalisation period. Bharat Coking Coal Limited was incorporated in 1972 to operate coking-coal mines including those of the Jharia Coalfield.

2009
MASTER PLAN / REHABILITATION

Jharia Master Plan

In August 2009, the Government approved a Master Plan addressing fire, subsidence, rehabilitation and the diversion of surface infrastructure in the Jharia and Raniganj coalfields.

2019
REMOTE SENSING / FIRE

Thermal mapping makes subsurface fire visible

Published thermal-infrared research used spaceborne data to detect and delineate subsurface coal-mine fire, adding a contemporary sensing layer to the archive.

2025
REVISED PLAN / PRESENT

Rehabilitation reconfigured

On 25 June 2025, the Cabinet approved the Revised Jharia Master Plan, focusing on fire, land subsidence and rehabilitation of affected families, with priority given to vulnerable sites.

THE ARCHIVE READS THESE EVENTS NOT AS ISOLATED DATES, BUT AS LAYERS THAT CONTINUE TO SHAPE LAND, MOVEMENT, MEMORY AND EVERYDAY LIFE.

SYNTHESIS / WHAT THE ARCHIVE REVEALS

Land can be mapped.
Fire can be detected.
Risk can be classified.
Families can be relocated.

But displacement also transforms memory, language, belonging and the meaning of home.

The archive therefore treats displacement not as a single movement between two locations, but as a continuing transformation of place.
06 / ABOUT THE ARCHIVE

Preserving more
than place.

What happens to a place when the ground beneath it can no longer be trusted?

The Digital Spatial-Linguistic Archive of Jharia is conceived as an experimental research interface for reading coal-fire-induced displacement through multiple forms of evidence.

Rather than treating displacement only as movement from one location to another, the archive considers transformations in landscape, settlement, memory, language and relationships with home.

ETHICS + LIMITATIONS

Reading with
uncertainty

This website is a curated digital archive and does not claim to be a complete record of displacement in Jharia. Spatial relationships shown in the interface should therefore not be interpreted as verified individual migration routes unless supported by source evidence.

Oral histories should only be incorporated with appropriate consent, attribution or anonymisation. Quotations, translations, local vocabulary, speaker identities and precise locations should not be invented where primary evidence is absent.

Selected sources

The archive draws on institutional documentation and published material. Each record should be read through its stated provenance, source and limitations.

ARCHIVAL READING

What does this record tell us?

This map provides a historical spatial layer through which Jharia was represented primarily as geological and mineral territory.

Within this archive, it becomes a point of comparison with later records of underground fire, unstable land, displacement and rehabilitation.

Reading these records together allows the landscape to be understood not as a fixed map, but as a territory whose meaning changes over time.

EVIDENCE ROLE

Establishes geological territory and provides an early spatial baseline for later fire and displacement evidence.

WHAT THIS RECORD CANNOT SHOW

Geological mapping establishes territory and extraction conditions but does not record resident memory or displacement experience.

LINGUISTIC READING

Who gets to name risk?

Policy does more than respond to the landscape. It produces categories through which land, settlements and populations become administratively legible.

Terms such as affected, endangered, unstable and rehabilitation therefore form part of the linguistic archive of Jharia.

EVIDENCE ROLE

Shows how environmental risk is translated into institutional categories, policy and rehabilitation language.

WHAT THIS RECORD CANNOT SHOW

Policy documentation records institutional categories and rehabilitation frameworks but cannot independently represent how affected residents understand home, loss or relocation.

ARCHIVE QUESTION INSTITUTIONAL LANGUAGE ↔ LIVED LANGUAGE

How does the language of planning differ from the language through which residents describe home, danger and displacement?

SPATIAL READING

Relocation is not simply movement.

A rehabilitation colony can be mapped as a destination, but displacement also changes access to work, social networks, infrastructure and relationships with the former home.

Belgaria therefore expands the archive from environmental risk toward the spatial and social consequences of resettlement.

EVIDENCE ROLE

Documents the geography and institutional context of rehabilitation and resettlement at Belgaria.

WHAT THIS RECORD CANNOT SHOW

Resettlement documentation establishes rehabilitation geography and institutional context, but it does not by itself reconstruct an individual family's complete displacement journey.
